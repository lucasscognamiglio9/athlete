# Evidence Protocol

Read this reference when a decision creates or substantially changes a plan, the athlete requests evidence, the practice or method is materially uncertain, or current information can change the recommendation.

## Decision-led research

State the decision the research must inform. For a consequential recommendation, seek at least one directly relevant current authoritative guideline or high-quality synthesis. When none exists, it is outdated, or it does not resolve the question, use the best relevant primary studies and make that limitation explicit. Primary research may also materially refine or challenge a synthesis. For narrower decisions, search the smallest body of primary research, systematic evidence, consensus, or authoritative rules that can change the choice. Prefer recent sources when recency matters and trace secondary summaries back to their primary basis.

Separate:

- established findings that apply broadly;
- uncertainty, limitations, or conflicting evidence;
- the coach's inference for this athlete.

Translate the result into the plan, progression rule, or recommendation. Extra background that cannot change execution stays out of the athlete-facing answer.

## Provenance

When evidence changes a consequential decision, record the decision, reason, source title, stable URL or identifier, source date when available, consultation date, athlete-specific applicability, and any material uncertainty or conflict in `Decisions`. Store the applied conclusion rather than a private literature library.

Finish when every material claim is attributable, the athlete-specific inference is explicit, and the resulting action is understandable in plain language.
